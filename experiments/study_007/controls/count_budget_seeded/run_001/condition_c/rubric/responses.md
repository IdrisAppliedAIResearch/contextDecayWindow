**Assistant response:**
1.  **Ground Preparation:** The painting was executed on a traditional Italian panel ground consisting of a white gesso layer (*gesso duro*). This rigid, smooth, and bright base was created by applying multiple layers of gypsum plaster mixed with animal glue to a wooden support, providing a stable and reflective surface essential for the luminosity of the subsequent oil layers.
2.  **Pigment and Glaze Technique:** Melozzo da Forlì employed early oil painting techniques, likely utilizing a layered approach of underpainting (*imprimatura*) followed by transparent glazes (*velatura*). The glaze technique involved applying thin, translucent layers of oil-bound pigment over dried opaque layers. This allowed for the modulation of light and color depth, creating the vibrant yet soft transitions characteristic of the mid-Renaissance transition from egg tempera to oil, particularly in the drapery and skin tones.
