3. **Support Movement and Wood Pathology:**
The wooden supports (typically poplar, though oak and pine were also used) are subject to warping, cupping, and twisting due to uneven moisture exchange. 4. **Cleaning and Solvent Sensitivity:**
Tempera paint is water-soluble to some degree and highly sensitive to alkaline or strong solvent cleaners. Historical cleaning often used harsh soaps or alkaline solutions that dissolved the original binder or faded sensitive pigments. Successful modern conservation uses mild, pH-neutral surfactants and localized solvent gels to clean varnish without affecting the paint layer. The least successful methods were "wet cleaning" with uncontrolled water application, which could swell the wood support and cause the gesso to lift.

5. **Varnish and Surface Treatments:**
The application of protective varnishes was common but problematic. Natural resin varnishes yellow and become insoluble over time. Historical attempts to remove old varnish using strong solvents (like turpentine or alcohol) often removed thin layers of original glaze or pigment. Successful interventions involve the use of emulsions or gel-based cleaners that allow for controlled, slow removal of degraded varnish while protecting the underlying tempera. The most successful long-term strategy is now the avoidance of new varnishes whenever possible, or the use of synthetic, non-yellowing resins (like Paraloid B-72) that can be safely removed in the future.
